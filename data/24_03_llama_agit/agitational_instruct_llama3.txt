Parallax is an astronomical method that determines distance by comparing trigonometric measurements from different points of view. It measures distances to stars or other celestial bodies as they appear to move in relation to more distant stars, which are assumed to be at such a great distance that their own expected movement due to Earth's rotation and its annual motion around the Sun can be ignored.  The position of a nearby star will shift very slightly against the background sky over six months (half an orbit) when viewed from two positions on opposite sides of earth's path through space. The difference between those angles provides parallax information indicating how far away the star really is.
