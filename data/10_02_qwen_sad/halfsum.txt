Dark matter is inferred from astronomical measurements that reveal a deficit of visible mass, manifesting as discrepancies between the observed dynamics and luminosity. Its existence was first proposed in 1933 by Swiss astrophysicist Fritz Zwicky to explain why galaxies rotate at speeds inconsistent with Newtonian gravity's predictions based on their emitted light alone. The hypothesis gained credence through numerous subsequent observations across various cosmic scales. Current estimates suggest that dark matter constitutes approximately six times more mass than all ordinary (baryonic) matter combined within observable space; however, its composition remains unknown. This enigma has sparked extensive research into exotic subatomic particles.
